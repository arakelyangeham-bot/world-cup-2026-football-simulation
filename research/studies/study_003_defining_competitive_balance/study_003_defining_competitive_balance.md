# Study 003 — Defining Competitive Balance

### An Empirical Comparison of Pre-Match Strength Representations

---

# Abstract

Competitive balance is one of the most fundamental concepts in football.

Intuitively, closely matched teams appear to produce different kinds of matches than teams with large strength differences.

However, the concept of competitive balance itself is rarely defined explicitly.

Different rating systems represent team strength in different ways, including FIFA rankings, attack ratings, defensive ratings, goalkeeper quality, and expected-goal models.

The purpose of this study is not to determine which representation produces the most accurate predictions.

Instead, the objective is to investigate which representations best explain observable properties of football.

Using the Football Observatory developed in Study 001, this study compares multiple pre-match strength representations against a common set of football observables.

Rather than drawing strong conclusions from a relatively small historical dataset, the study establishes an evidence-based methodology for evaluating football representations and generates hypotheses for future investigation.

---

# 1. Introduction

Football matches are influenced by differences in team strength.

However, "team strength" is not a single quantity.

Different models emphasize different aspects of football.

Examples include:

- FIFA rating points
- Attacking quality
- Defensive quality
- Goalkeeper quality
- Expected-goal strength

The central question of this study is therefore:

> Which representations of pre-match strength best explain observable football?

This study treats competitive balance as a measurable scientific concept rather than an assumed property.

---

# 2. Research Question

Which pre-match strength representations exhibit the strongest empirical relationships with observable football outcomes?

---

# 3. Data

Historical international match dataset.

389 matches.

Pre-match variables:

- FIFA points difference
- Attack difference
- Midfield difference
- Defense difference
- Goalkeeper difference
- Poisson attack difference
- Poisson defense difference

Football observables:

- Draw
- One-goal match
- Clean sheet
- Both teams scored
- High-scoring match

All analyses use quantile-based response curves together with Wilson confidence intervals.

---

# 4. Methodology

For every representation:

```text
Representation
        │
        ▼
Football Observable
        │
        ▼
Response Curve
        │
        ▼
Observable Relationship
```

Representations were intentionally analyzed independently.

No composite strength measures were introduced during this study.

The purpose was descriptive rather than predictive.

---

# 5. Results

## Observation 5.1 — Draw Behaviour

### Observation

No single representation produced a clearly dominant response curve for draw frequency.

### Evidence

Response ranges were broadly similar across the tested strength representations.

Confidence intervals overlapped substantially.

### Hypothesis

Draw behaviour may depend on multiple interacting dimensions of team strength rather than a single scalar representation.

### Conclusion

No conclusion is warranted at the current sample size.

Further investigation using richer datasets is recommended.

---

## Observation 5.2 — One-Goal Matches

### Observation

One-goal match frequency varied across representations but no single variable consistently dominated.

### Evidence

Observed response ranges were comparable.

Sampling uncertainty remains substantial.

### Hypothesis

One-goal football may emerge from match dynamics rather than static pre-match balance alone.

### Conclusion

Further research required.

---

## Observation 5.3 — Clean Sheets

### Observation

Clean-sheet behaviour showed measurable variation across representations.

### Evidence

Some representations appeared more informative than others.

Confidence intervals remain wide.

### Hypothesis

Goalkeeper and defensive representations may contribute more strongly than global rating systems.

### Conclusion

Requires larger datasets before interpretation.

---

## Observation 5.4 — Both Teams Scored

### Observation

Midfield difference produced the largest observed response range among the tested representations.

### Evidence

This relationship exceeded that observed using FIFA points difference, although uncertainty remains substantial.

### Hypothesis

Midfield balance may influence reciprocal attacking opportunities more strongly than overall rating difference.

### Conclusion

This represents the strongest candidate hypothesis generated by the present study but should not yet be interpreted as an established football law.

---

## Observation 5.5 — High-Scoring Matches

### Observation

High-scoring matches varied with several representations.

### Evidence

No single representation demonstrated overwhelming explanatory power.

### Hypothesis

Goal environment may depend on multiple offensive and defensive dimensions simultaneously.

### Conclusion

Future composite representations should be investigated.

---

# 6. General Discussion

The primary result of this study is methodological rather than predictive.

The Football Observatory successfully distinguished relationships between multiple representations of pre-match strength and observable football outcomes.

Importantly, different football observables appeared to respond differently to different representations.

This suggests that competitive balance should not necessarily be viewed as a single scalar quantity.

Instead, competitive balance may be multidimensional.

Different aspects of football may depend upon different representations of team quality.

---

# 7. Limitations

This study should be interpreted conservatively.

Current limitations include:

- 389 historical matches
- No event-level observations
- No player-level information
- No tactical information
- No temporal match dynamics

Consequently, all conclusions should be considered exploratory.

---

# 8. Implications for the Football Match Generator

The study suggests that future versions of the match generator should avoid relying on a single universal notion of competitive balance.

Instead, different football processes may ultimately depend upon different representations.

Examples include:

- Draw generation
- Goal environment
- Both teams scored
- Clean-sheet probability

These questions remain open.

# What We Learned

- Competitive balance is measurable.
- Football observables respond differently to different pre-match representations.
- The Football Observatory can compare competing football representations.
- The current dataset is sufficient for exploratory empirical football research.
- Confidence intervals are essential for responsible interpretation.

---

# What We Still Do Not Know

The present study leaves several important questions unanswered.

We do not yet know:

- whether richer datasets would strengthen the observed relationships,
- whether event-level information changes the conclusions,
- whether composite strength representations outperform individual variables,
- whether football representations should differ for different match processes,
- how these relationships evolve during the course of a match.

These questions motivate the next phase of Computational Football research.

---

# Conclusion

Study 003 represents the first empirical comparison of competing football representations within the Football Observatory framework.

Rather than identifying a single "best" representation of competitive balance, the study suggests that different observable aspects of football may depend upon different representations of pre-match team strength.

This result motivates a broader view of football simulation in which individual football processes are informed by the representations most appropriate for that specific phenomenon.

More importantly, the study demonstrates that the Football Observatory is capable of generating new football hypotheses directly from historical data.

This establishes the Observatory as a genuine empirical research instrument rather than simply a collection of analysis scripts.
