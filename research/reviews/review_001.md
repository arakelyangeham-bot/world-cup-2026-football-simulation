# Review 001

# Foundations of Competitive Football

### A Synthesis of Studies 003–007

---

# Abstract

This review summarizes the first body of empirical research conducted using the Football Observatory.

Rather than presenting new analyses, the purpose of this review is to synthesize the findings of the initial Observatory studies into a coherent picture of competitive football.

The review consolidates observations regarding competitive balance, one-goal football, draw football, equilibrium football, and competitive resolution.

It also identifies the principal unanswered questions that motivate the next phase of Computational Football research.

---

# 1. Introduction

The Football Observatory was created to answer a simple question:

> How does football actually behave?

Studies 001–007 established the methodology and applied it to several fundamental football populations.

This review integrates those findings.

---

# 2. Methodological Contributions

The Observatory now provides four complementary research instruments.

## Football Relationships

Measure how football observables respond to changing pre-match conditions.

Examples:

- FIFA gap → Draw rate
- Attack gap → BTTS

Examples:

---

## Football Population Comparisons

Directly compare football populations.

Examples:

---

## Comparative Football Studies

Investigate competing football populations.

Example:

Competitive Resolution

---

# 3. Scientific Findings

## Finding 1

Competitive football separates naturally into multiple football populations.

Examples include:

- One-goal football
- Draw football

---

## Finding 2

Draw football itself separates into:

- Scoreless equilibrium
- Scoring equilibrium

This suggests that draws are not a homogeneous football population.

---

## Finding 3

Different football observables appear to respond differently to different pre-match representations.

No single representation dominated every football phenomenon.

This suggests that competitive balance is multidimensional.

---

## Finding 4

Football populations possess measurable fingerprints.

Examples include:

- average goals
- clean sheets
- BTTS
- goal difference
- common scorelines

Football populations therefore appear to represent meaningful scientific objects rather than arbitrary collections of matches.

---

## Finding 5

The Observatory consistently generated new research questions from empirical observations.

This demonstrates that the research methodology is self-sustaining.

---

# 4. Engineering Implications

No study produced sufficient evidence to justify immediate changes to the production match generator.

However, several studies generated hypotheses worthy of future investigation.

These hypotheses should remain in the research branch until independently supported.

---

# 5. Current Football Knowledge

The following observations now possess empirical support.

## Competitive football

Competitive football resolves through multiple observable populations.

---

## One-goal football

One-goal football is not synonymous with defensive football.

---

## Draw football

Draw football contains multiple equilibrium modes.

---

## Competitive balance

Competitive balance appears to be multidimensional rather than adequately represented by a single scalar variable.

---

# 6. Outstanding Questions

The Observatory has identified several unanswered questions.

## Mechanism Questions

What mechanisms produce:

- competitive resolution?
- defensive separation?
- mutual scoring?

---

## Representation Questions

Which pre-match representations best explain:

- draw football?
- one-goal football?
- clean sheets?

---

## Event-Level Questions

How do:

- tempo,
- possession,
- substitutions,
- game state,

contribute to these football populations?

Current data cannot answer these questions.

---

# 7. Transition to Phase 5B

The first Observatory studies were primarily descriptive.

The next phase should investigate football mechanisms rather than football populations.

Examples include:

- Competitive Resolution Mechanisms
- Defensive Separation
- Mutual Scoring
- Offensive Escalation
- Goal Environment

The Observatory infrastructure is now sufficiently mature to support these investigations without significant architectural modification.

---

# 8. Conclusions

The first Observatory studies achieved three major objectives.

1. They established a reproducible methodology for empirical football research.

2. They demonstrated that football populations possess measurable statistical structure.

3. They generated a sustainable research workflow in which completed studies naturally produce the next research questions.

The Computational Football project has therefore transitioned from building software infrastructure to building football knowledge.

Future progress is expected to arise primarily from improved understanding of football rather than additional software architecture.

---

# Lessons Learned

Looking back over the first Observatory investigations, one lesson stands out.

The project advances most effectively when historical football determines the research agenda.

Rather than beginning with modeling ideas, studies should begin with observable football phenomena.

The Observatory should remain the primary source of future research questions.

---

# Future Direction

The next chapter of Computational Football should investigate football mechanisms.

The objective is no longer merely to describe football.

The objective is to explain football.

This review therefore concludes the foundational descriptive phase of the Observatory and marks the beginning of explanatory Computational Football research.
