# Competition Research Program A

## Objective

Establish a computational methodology for comparing football competition formats.

---

## Experiments

031A
Research Framework Validation

031B
Synthetic League vs Knockout

031C
Production League vs Knockout

---

## Major Findings

### Finding 1

The Version 3 Research Framework successfully supports reproducible computational football experiments.

### Finding 2

League competitions consistently identified stronger champions than knockout competitions across both football models.

### Finding 3

Production Football Model v1 reduced upset frequency while preserving the qualitative competition result.

---

## Methodological Lessons

- Controlled experiments are feasible.
- Football model and competition structure should remain separate variables.
- Replication studies are valuable.
- Research artifacts should be treated as first-class project outputs.

---

## New Questions

Experiment 031 generated several new research questions:

- Why did upset rates decrease?
- Which football-model component produced the change?
- Does repository choice matter?
- Does the conclusion hold for larger tournaments?

---

## Program Conclusion

Competition Research Program A successfully demonstrated that the project can produce reproducible computational football research.

Future research should build upon this methodology rather than redesign it.
